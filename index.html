STYLISH KEYBOARD PRIVACY POLICY

Effective date: November 26, 2026

This privacy policy explains what information the Stylish Keyboard app for iPhone and iPad (the "app") and the Stylish Keyboard keyboard that comes with it (the "keyboard") use, why, where it is kept, who it is shared with, and the choices you have. "We", "us" and "our" mean Shubham Mahato, the developer of Stylish Keyboard.

We wrote this policy to be read, not skimmed past. If anything is unclear, write to us at contactshubhamtripz@gmail.com.

THE SHORT VERSION

  • What you type stays on your phone. The keyboard never sends, logs, shares or sells what you type, with or without Full Access.
  • The keyboard works fully without Full Access and without an internet connection. Full Access only turns on optional extras, such as the clipboard, stickers and key sounds.
  • The keyboard itself has no analytics, no ads and no trackers, and it makes no network requests.
  • The app (not the keyboard) sends usage statistics and crash reports, not linked to who you are, to Google Firebase, so we can see which features are used and fix crashes. You can turn each off at any time in the app: Settings → Privacy.
  • The app also downloads its own settings from Google Firebase (for example, to tell you when an update is needed), word lists for languages you type in English letters from our word list server, and sticker packs from our sticker server.
  • There are no accounts, no ads, no tracking across apps or websites, and we never sell your information.
  • What you save in the app (your words, copies, details and photo theme) stays on your device, and you can see and delete it in the app.

1. WHAT THE KEYBOARD DOES WITH WHAT YOU TYPE

A keyboard sees what you type, so we built Stylish Keyboard so that your typing never leaves your device.

iOS never gives custom keyboards password fields: when you type a password, iOS switches to Apple's own keyboard. In other private fields (such as one-time codes and card numbers, and fields that turn autocorrection off), the keyboard does not learn from what you type and its extra features stay off.

To work, the keyboard reads a small part of the text around the cursor, as iOS shares it, and only in the device's memory:

  • Suggestions, autocorrection and next-word predictions: the text before the cursor.
  • The emoji row (for example, "pizza" shows 🍕): the word you are typing or just typed.
  • Typing animations and the keyboard buddy's reactions (for example, "happy birthday" plays an animation): the last 48 characters before the cursor, at the end of a word or after a letter, only while these features are on.
  • Chips above the keys, such as a sum's answer ("450/3=") or your details after "my number": the last 32 characters before the cursor, never in private fields.

This text is looked at on your device and then forgotten. It is never stored, logged, sent to us or shared with anyone.

What the keyboard does keep on your device:

  • Learned words: the keyboard learns the words you type (and which word tends to follow which), so its suggestions fit how you write. This is on by default and can be turned off in the app (Settings → Typing → Learn new words). You can clear everything it learned at any time (Settings → Learned words → Clear learned words). It never learns from private fields.
  • Your recent emoji, recent stickers, the emoji skin tones you pick, the font style and typing language you choose in the keyboard.

The keyboard also uses Apple's list of your text replacements and contact names (which iOS gives keyboards without access to your contacts), only on your device, to suggest those words. It is never stored or sent.

2. FULL ACCESS

iOS keeps custom keyboards in a strict sandbox. "Allow Full Access" (Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards → Stylish Keyboard) lets a keyboard do more, and iOS shows a strong warning when you turn it on, because it would let a keyboard send data over the internet. Stylish Keyboard does not send what you type, with or without Full Access. Turning Full Access on is not permission for us to collect your typing, and we don't.

The keyboard works fully without Full Access. With it, these optional features work:

  • Key sounds and haptic feedback.
  • The clipboard panel, the Edit text panel (copy, cut and paste) and Recent copies (see section 3).
  • Stickers: tapping a sticker copies its picture so you can paste it in a chat (iOS lets keyboards type only text).
  • Smart paste: a "Paste link" chip when you have just copied something. To offer it, iOS tells the keyboard what kind of text you copied (a link, an email, a number) without the keyboard reading it; the copy is read only when you tap the chip.
  • Location sharing (see section 4).
  • Settings and lists shared with the app: for example, the list of learned words the app shows you, and a note that the keyboard was opened (a time, no text) so the app's setup screen knows the keyboard works.
  • Usage events from the keyboard: which keyboard features and tools were used (for example, "the clipboard panel was opened"), kept on your device for the app to send with its usage statistics (see section 4). Never what you typed or copied.

You can turn Full Access off at any time in the same iOS setting. The keyboard keeps working.

3. INFORMATION KEPT ON YOUR DEVICE

The app and the keyboard keep the following on your device, in their own storage and in a storage area they share on your device. None of it is sent to us.

  • Your settings: theme, languages, layouts, sounds, the emoji row, the keyboard buddy and typing animations.
  • Learned words (see section 1) and your personal dictionary (words you add in the app).
  • Recent copies (on by default, with Full Access): the last 20 texts you copied, each for 24 hours, so you can paste them again from the clipboard panel. Copies that a password manager marks as private are never kept. You can delete one or all of them in the clipboard panel or in the app (Settings → Recent copies), or turn the feature off.
  • Quick replies: messages you send often, edited in the app (Settings → Quick replies).
  • My details: your name, phone number, email addresses and home and work addresses, only the ones you add in the app (Settings → My details), so the keyboard can offer them in fields that ask for them. You can change or remove each one there.
  • Word lists for your languages: if you type in English letters in a language such as Kannada or Tamil with English (India) on, its word list, downloaded by the app (see section 4e). Turning the language off removes it.
  • Downloaded sticker packs: extra stickers the app downloads (see section 4f), so the keyboard's and the app's Stickers can show them. They contain no information about you.
  • Your photo theme: a copy of the photo you pick, sized for the keyboard. The app gets only that photo from your library (iOS's photo picker needs no access to your library). Removing the photo theme deletes the copy.
  • Small app records: whether setup is done, which prompts you have seen, a random number used to roll new features out gradually, when the app last downloaded its settings, and how many times an update prompt was shown.

This information is included in your device's own backup (iCloud or your computer), like other app data, under your control. Deleting the app deletes all of it from your device.

4. INFORMATION THAT LEAVES YOUR DEVICE

Only the following leaves your device, and only from the app, never from the keyboard.

a) Usage statistics (Google Firebase Analytics)

On by default; turn off in the app: Settings → Privacy → Share usage statistics.

What is sent: a random app-instance identifier created by Firebase for this installation; app opens and sessions; which features, tools, settings and themes of the app and the keyboard are used, and our own identifiers for them (for example, "theme applied: Pop Paper"); the app version, device model, iOS version, language and region; and your approximate location (such as your country and region), which Google works out from your internet address (Google says it does not keep the address).

What is never sent: anything you type, paste or search for, which app you type in, or which sticker you send.

Usage events from the keyboard are kept on your device (only with Full Access) and sent by the app the next time you open it, with the day they happened. With Share usage statistics off, nothing is collected and the keyboard's kept events are deleted unsent.

b) Crash reports (Google Firebase Crashlytics)

On by default; turn off in the app: Settings → Privacy → Send crash reports.

What is sent when the app crashes: where in our code it crashed, the device model, iOS version, app version, and free memory and storage at the time. Crash reports caught while the switch is off are deleted, never sent.

c) The app's settings (Google Firebase Remote Config)

The app downloads small settings from us, such as which app versions need an update, and switches that turn a feature on or off. To pick the right settings, Firebase receives a random installation identifier, the app's version, the iOS version, your device's language, region and time zone, and, when usage statistics have set them, the app's applied theme and languages. The app needs these settings to work correctly (for example, to tell you about a required update), so this happens whether or not usage statistics are on. It happens only from the app, at most about once an hour while you use it. Nothing about you comes back, only our settings.

d) Your location, only when you ask (Apple)

With Full Access and iOS's location permission, tapping Location in the keyboard's menu reads your location once and types your address into your message. To turn the coordinates into an address, iOS sends them to Apple's address service, under Apple's privacy policy. Your location is never kept and never sent to us. If Precise Location is off, only your area is typed.

e) Word lists for your languages (our word list server)

If English (India) and a language such as Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali or Marathi are both on, the app downloads that language's word list, so the keyboard can suggest its words typed in English letters. The app asks our word list server (hosted on GitHub Pages) for a small index file and the list. These are plain file requests: nothing about you, your typing or your device is sent with them. Like any web request, the server sees your device's internet address. This happens only from the app, only when a list is missing or a day has passed, never from the keyboard.

f) Sticker packs (our sticker server)

The app downloads extra sticker packs (Microsoft's Fluent Emoji 3D pictures, credited in the app's Acknowledgements) so the keyboard can offer them. The app asks our sticker server (hosted on GitHub Pages) for a small index file and the packs it lists. These are plain file requests: nothing about you, your typing or your device is sent with them. Like any web request, the server sees your device's internet address. This happens only from the app, when it opens, at most every few hours, and a pack only when it is new or updated, never from the keyboard.

What we never collect: your name, email address, phone number or contacts; the text you type, copy or paste; your passwords; your photos (beyond the one copy you choose for a theme, which stays on your device); your precise location; or the advertising identifier (IDFA). The app has no sign-in.

The analytics we use does not use the advertising identifier, does not collect the identifier for vendors (IDFV), and has advertising features turned off.

5. HOW WE USE INFORMATION

We use the information in section 4 only to:

  • see which features are used, so we can improve them and decide what to build next;
  • find and fix crashes;
  • send the app its settings, such as update notices and switching a broken feature off.

We do not use it for advertising, we do not build profiles of you, we do not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and we do not sell it. The keyboard collects your activity only to make the keyboard itself work better, on your device.

6. WHO WE SHARE INFORMATION WITH

  • Google (Firebase), our service provider for usage statistics, crash reports and the app's settings, processes the information in section 4 on our behalf, under its terms for Firebase. Google is required by those terms to protect this information at least as well as this policy does. Google's privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy — Firebase and privacy: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy
  • Apple, when you use location sharing (section 4d) and when the app opens the App Store page to update.
  • GitHub, which hosts our word list and sticker servers (sections 4e and 4f), receives plain file requests and sends the lists and packs. We send it nothing about you.
  • When required by law: we may disclose information if a valid legal request requires it.

We share no information with advertisers, data brokers or anyone else, and we do not sell or "share" personal information as those words are used in California law.

7. PERMISSIONS

  • Location (while using the app): asked only the first time you tap Location in the keyboard's menu, or tap Allow location in the app. Used only to type your address into your message. You can change it at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Stylish Keyboard.
  • Full Access: see section 2.
  • We do not ask for access to your contacts, photo library, microphone, camera or notifications, and we do not ask to track you.

8. YOUR CHOICES

  • Usage statistics and crash reports: turn either off in the app, Settings → Privacy. This takes effect at once.
  • Full Access: turn it off in iOS Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards → Stylish Keyboard.
  • Learned words: turn learning off (Settings → Typing) or clear them (Settings → Learned words).
  • Recent copies, quick replies, My details, your personal dictionary and photo theme: view, change or delete them in the app.
  • Location: change the permission in iOS Settings, or simply don't use Location sharing.
  • Remove the keyboard: iOS Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards → Edit.
  • Delete the app: this removes everything the app and keyboard kept on your device.

9. HOW LONG INFORMATION IS KEPT, AND DELETING IT

  • On your device: until you delete it in the app, turn the feature off, or delete the app. Recent copies are deleted 24 hours after they were last seen.
  • Usage statistics: the individual records are kept by Google for up to 14 months, then deleted automatically. Reports that only show totals (for example, how many people used a feature) may be kept longer; they say nothing about any one person.
  • Crash reports: kept by Google for up to 90 days.
  • The app's settings: we keep nothing about you; Firebase uses the identifier described in section 4c only to send the settings.

The information Firebase receives is not linked to your name, email address or Apple Account, so we usually cannot tell which data is yours. You can stop all collection at once with the switches in Settings → Privacy, and deleting the app removes the random identifiers from your device (a new installation gets new ones). To ask us to delete or show data about you, write to contactshubhamtripz@gmail.com; we will answer within 30 days and delete whatever we can find, and we may ask for the app-instance identifier to find it.

10. SECURITY

Information that leaves your device is sent over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Information on your device is protected by iOS's data protection. We collect as little as we can, and we run no servers of our own that store your information.

11. CHILDREN

Stylish Keyboard is not directed to children under 13 (or the minimum age in your country). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. The usage statistics and crash reports described above are not linked to anyone's identity, and a parent can turn them off in Settings → Privacy. If you believe a child has given us personal information, write to contactshubhamtripz@gmail.com and we will delete it.
